1. **fed-01; purposes P1–P4 — HIGH.** An express purpose is missing. Section 2(3) says the Act is “also intended to complement and not replace existing procedures” and “is not intended to limit in any way access” to normally available government information.

2. **fed-01; provision V61, s. 48 — HIGH.** Listed as creating a power, but it allocates evidential burdens. Section 48(1): “the burden of establishing … is on the government institution concerned.” Neither subsection creates the listed power; the table expressly excludes evidence rules.

3. **fed-01; provision V96, s. 71.14 — HIGH.** Finality is incorrectly treated as an independently created power. Section 71.14 says a determination “is final for the purposes of this Part.” The equivalent s. 90.24 is excluded expressly as a “finality clause.”

4. **fed-01; excluded s. 45 — HIGH.** The reason “creates no offence, duty, power or right” overlooks mandatory adjudication procedure: an application “is to be heard and determined in a summary way.” Compare fed-02’s inclusion of CSIS Act s. 27 because applications “shall be heard in private.”

5. **fed-01; V95 / excluded s. 71.13 — MEDIUM.** Comparable publication exemptions receive opposite treatment. Section 71.12 is included where the Speaker determines publication would breach privilege; s. 71.13 is excluded where the Speaker determines publication “could compromise security.” Both depend on the Speaker’s determination.

6. **fed-01; text file, schedules — MEDIUM.** Schedule contents survive, but their identifying headings do not. This matters because s. 3 refers to institutions “listed in Schedule I,” while s. 24(1) refers to provisions “set out in Schedule II.” The file instead jumps into institution names and later an unlabelled “Act Provision” table. This establishes lost structure, not missing schedule entries.

7. **fed-02; excluded coordinating amendments, s. 34 — HIGH.** “Coordinating amendments” is not a defensible blanket exclusion of substantive duties. Section 34’s replacement Criminal Code s. 810.3(4) requires officials to “cause all samples of bodily substances … to be destroyed”; replacement s. 810.4 prohibits unauthorized use and disclosure: “No person shall use” and “no person shall use, disclose or allow the disclosure.” Neither substantive section is listed.

8. **fed-02; omitted s. 53 / IRPA s. 74(d) — HIGH.** The amended appeal entitlement and its conditions are neither listed nor defensibly excluded. Section 74(d), as replaced by s. 53: “an appeal to the Federal Court of Appeal may be made only if” the judge certifies a serious question of general importance.

9. **fed-02; text file, schedules — HIGH.** Schedule identities have been stripped, obscuring substantive scope. Section 2 enacts “Schedules 1 to 3”; enacted SCISA s. 2 distinguishes institutions excluded through “Schedule 1” from recipients “listed in Schedule 2.” The file ends with an unlabelled Commissioner’s office and recipient/head table, without those schedule headings.

10. **fed-03; purpose P1 — MEDIUM.** The accommodation object is left unsplit: s. 2 combines making one’s life with “and to have their needs accommodated.” That differs from splitting “and to enable” in fed-01 and “and to maintain” in fed-04. The table’s shared-qualifier explanation does not eliminate the distinct accommodation object.

11. **fed-03; provision V2 / exclusion “all others” — MEDIUM.** The scope rationale is inconsistent. Section 54 is included because it supplies remedies for s. 13, but s. 57 is excluded wholesale as outside the entry, although it expressly provides enforcement of those remedies: “An order under section 53 or 54 may … be made an order of the Federal Court.” Either V2 exceeds a strict s. 13 scope, or the exclusion needs a defensible distinction.

12. **fed-04; provisions V9–V10, ss. 86–87 — HIGH.** The corpus names ss. 77–85, not all of Division 9. These additional provisions authorize applications “during an admissibility hearing, a detention review or an appeal” (s. 86(1)) and “in the course of a judicial review” (s. 87(1)). Their inclusion contradicts the table’s stated section-entry limitation.

13. **fed-04; text file — HIGH.** The file contains preliminary provisions plus Part 1, although the corpus entry names sections, not a Part. That satisfies neither alternative in approved rule 3. Its endpoint is s. 94, requiring a report “on the operation of this Act”; it is a selected portion of the Act. The draft’s added “Part that contains them” exception is not in the approved rule.

14. **fed-04; SCOPE header — LOW.** “Preliminary provisions (ss. 1–9)” understates what is included before Part 1. Section 10 is also present: “The Minister must consult with the governments of the provinces” (s. 10(2)).

15. **fed-05; text file — HIGH.** The file appears complete for its declared Part III, but the corpus names s. 95, not Part III. Thus it breaks approved rule 3’s requirement of the whole Act or Parts actually named by the entry. The text begins with s. 84, “In this Part,” and ends with s. 117.15. No separate defect was found in V1 or the hand-written deterrence purpose within that supplied text.

16. **prov-01; purpose P1 — MEDIUM.** The hand-written limb “To regulate aggressive solicitation” names the regulatory mechanism, despite the note saying it names none. Section 2(2) does exactly that: “No person shall solicit in an aggressive manner.” That limb is substantially satisfied by regulation itself; the separate public-safety limb remains independently assessable.

17. **prov-02; excluded s. 17 — HIGH.** “Interpretation rule” overlooks an express power in the second sentence: “an institution may, on its own initiative, remove or alter an immovable or such movable property.”

18. **prov-02; excluded s. 21 — HIGH.** The grouped exclusion wrongly treats this amendment as non-operative. Section 21(1) inserts: “This Act imposes … a duty of religious neutrality on personnel members of public bodies.” It expressly imposes a duty.

19. **prov-02; V16 / excluded s. 9 — MEDIUM.** Exemptions are classified inconsistently. Section 31 is included as an acquired right, although it begins “Section 6 does not apply.” Section 9 is excluded merely as an exception: “Section 8 does not apply to persons whose face is covered for health reasons or because of a handicap.” The different treatment is not explained by their operative form.

20. **prov-03 — no finding.** No purpose clause or preamble was found; the protective hand-written purpose does not prescribe an enforcement mechanism. Operative ss. 2–5 are represented, including s. 5: “The Lieutenant Governor in Council may make regulations.” The supplied text appears complete.

21. **prov-04; provisions V6–V7 — HIGH.** Sections 57–58 exceed the entry’s named ss. 18–26 and contradict the table’s express section-entry limitation. They genuinely create powers—“The director may … amend the director’s pleadings” (s. 57(1)); “the court may draw an adverse inference” (s. 58(2))—but relatedness does not satisfy that limitation.

22. **prov-04; purpose P1 — MEDIUM.** “To provide civil remedies to deprive persons of property” retains the forfeiture mechanism as its purpose. Section 6(1) supplies precisely that remedy: “the court must make an order forfeiting to the government.” Removing the express UWO reference has not removed the underlying circularity.

23. **prov-04; flag asserting no criminal-use protection — MEDIUM.** The categorical assertion overlooks qualified protections. Section 68(1)(b) covers information obtained “pursuant to an order of the court”; s. 68(2) says the director “must not use or disclose specified information except as authorized”; s. 68(4) restricts compelled evidence. These are not absolute criminal-use immunity, but they contradict an assertion that no protection exists.

24. **prov-05; excluded s. 16 — HIGH.** “Not operative” ignores a prohibition in subsection (2): “A corporation shall not carry on any activity or exercise any power” restricted by its articles.

25. **prov-05; excluded s. 20 — HIGH.** This section creates obligations, rights and powers. Section 20(1) makes a contracting person “personally bound by the contract” and “entitled to the benefits”; s. 20(3) provides that “the court may make any order it thinks fit.” Its exclusion is plainly wrong.

26. **prov-05; excluded s. 100 — HIGH.** “Form of records” is only the opening subject. Section 100(2) imposes a substantive preservation duty: “A corporation and its agents shall take reasonable precautions to prevent the loss or destruction” of records.

27. **prov-05; excluded ss. 119–120 — HIGH.** Both are wrongly excluded as “Definition,” disregarding later subsections. Section 119(3) authorizes the court to appoint directors; s. 119(4) says the corporation “shall file articles of reorganization.” Section 120(2) says it “shall prepare a statement,” and s. 120(5) authorizes “any interim or final order” the court thinks fit.

28. **prov-05; excluded s. 142 — HIGH.** The deemed commencement date is subject to an express judicial power: “unless the court orders otherwise.” Calling the whole section non-operative overlooks that power.

29. **prov-05; excluded ss. 151 and 154 — HIGH.** These establish payment entitlements and liabilities. Section 151 makes winding-up expenses payable “in priority to all other claims”; s. 154 says contributory liability “creates a debt accruing due.” Neither is merely descriptive.

30. **prov-05; excluded s. 196 — HIGH.** The exclusion ignores judicial recourse in subsection (4): “a person may apply to the court for such order as the court thinks fit.”

31. **prov-05; excluded s. 202 — HIGH.** “Errors in certificates” contains several operative requirements and powers. Section 202(1) requires surrender of documents when requested; s. 202(3) says the Director “shall endorse a corrected certificate”; s. 202(5) provides an appeal to the Divisional Court.

32. **prov-05; excluded s. 203 — HIGH.** The section is not simply about record format. Section 203(2)(a) says the Director “shall give any copy required”; s. 203(4) permits public disclosure; ss. 203(5)–(7) authorize alternative filing requirements and retention of filings or search requests.

33. **prov-05; excluded s. 204 — HIGH.** The Director receives an express acceptance power: “the Director may accept a copy of it if it meets the Director’s requirements” (s. 204(1)). Deeming provisions elsewhere in the section do not eliminate that power.

34. **prov-05; excluded s. 207 — HIGH.** “Transition” overlooks operative subsections. Section 207(4) permits amendment of incorporating instruments; s. 207(5) prohibits restatement unless specified conditions hold: “A corporation shall not restate its articles … unless”.

35. **prov-05; excluded ss. 4.1, 197–199 — MEDIUM.** These permissions receive different treatment from included filing permissions such as V158, s. 204.1. Section 4.1 permits execution “in several documents”; s. 197(3) permits electronic delivery without recipient consent; s. 198 permits waiver or abridgement; s. 199(1) authorizes director/officer signatures. Their procedural character does not, without explanation, establish that they create none of the four.

36. **prov-05; excluded ss. 87, 113 and 206.3 — MEDIUM.** “Not operative” is questionable for provisions establishing ownership or vesting rights and liabilities. Section 87 says a corporation “owns any property” transferred to it; s. 113(c) vests “all the property, rights, privileges and franchises” in the amalgamated corporation; s. 206.3 makes filed records “the property of the Crown.”

37. **prov-05; provision V159, s. 204.2 — HIGH.** This section creates no identified power. Both subsections establish documentary priority: the electronic version “prevails over any other version.” The table excludes comparable interpretive/evidence rules elsewhere.

38. **prov-05; excluded s. 105 label — LOW.** Its “heading” is actually text from s. 104(2): notice “must set out the proposed amendment.” Section 105 itself says “Repealed.” The exclusion outcome is correct, but its attached label belongs to another section.

39. **muni-01; omitted §§ 608-3.1, 608-7.1 and 608-8.1 — HIGH.** None is listed or excluded. They respectively prohibit refusing entry with a service animal—“Refuse to permit such person to enter” (§ 608-3.1A(1)); discharging water or wastewater into a park—“No person shall cause or permit” (§ 608-7.1); and smoking near designated amenities—“no person shall smoke” (§ 608-8.1A).

40. **muni-01; omitted § 608-30.1 — HIGH.** An operative parking prohibition is absent: “No person shall park a motorized recreational vehicle in a park except in a designated area.”

41. **muni-01; omitted §§ 608-34.1–608-34.3 — HIGH.** All three create qualifying effects. Section 608-34.1A(1) prohibits control of “more than six dogs”; § 608-34.2B(2) requires “30 days’ written notice,” and C(1) permits an appeal; § 608-34.3D(2) delegates “authority to make final decisions” to the appeal committee.

42. **muni-02; omitted ss. 71J–71K — HIGH.** Neither is listed or excluded. Section 71J(1)(e) requires container identification: “mark and maintain … the name and telephone number of the owner.” Section 71K(1) provides that “the City Engineer may issue a permit,” while subsection (3) requires a written application.

43. **muni-02; text file — HIGH.** This is a selected combination of Part II and additional sections, not the whole bylaw or whole Parts named by the corpus entry. Statutory s. 2 identifies three Parts, but the file inserts “[Part I … omitted]” and omits Part III and ss. 99–101. Operative schedules are also absent: s. 67(3) refers to Schedule E “which is attached to and forms part of this By-law”; s. 71B(3)(g)(iii) depends on Schedule F.

44. **muni-03 — no finding.** Operative articles are represented once; art. 6.1 is defensibly excluded for territorial application/replacement. No purpose clause or preamble was found, and the hand-written purpose omits the itinerary/masking mechanisms. Article 1’s right is correctly included: “Toute personne a le droit d’utiliser et de jouir des voies, parcs et places publiques”.

45. **muni-05; text file — HIGH.** The “Whole by-law” claim conflicts with deliberately omitted schedules. Section 1.3 expressly says: “The schedules attached to this by-law form part of this by-law.” They affect operative requirements: s. 3.2(b)(i) uses the area outlined on Schedule E; s. 3.1(c) requires the symbol shown on Schedule D.

46. **muni-05; excluded ss. 2.5–2.6 — MEDIUM.** “Deeming rule” describes only part of each section. They also prescribe measurement procedure: sound “must be measured at the point of reception or at least 6.1 m” (§ 2.5) and “must be measured at least 6.1 m” (§ 2.6). Exclusion as an evidence rule is arguable, but the stated reason overlooks mandatory wording.

47. **muni-05; excluded s. 4.3, compared with muni-02 V29 — MEDIUM.** Permitted conduct is classified inconsistently. Section 4.3’s “a person may make, cause or permit” otherwise excessive noise is excluded as an exception; muni-02 s. 77B’s “A person may ride an electric kick scooter” is included as a power. The table supplies no consistent distinction.

The table does not yet reliably apply the approved rules. The purported verbatim purposes match the supplied text after whitespace normalization, but purpose coverage and splitting have defects. More serious problems are omitted operative sections, exclusions based only on opening headings, inconsistent provision scope, and incomplete or structurally damaged statute files. The clearest clean entries are prov-03 and muni-03. These conclusions concern only the supplied files; their claimed completeness was tested internally, not against external originals.
