## Draft letter

To the Honorable Rebecca Bauer-Kahan, Chair, and Members of the Assembly
Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection:

**Re: SB 574 (Umberg) — Generative Artificial Intelligence: Attorneys and Arbitrators**

I write in support of SB 574's core aims — confidentiality, accuracy,
fairness, transparency, and personal accountability in AI-assisted legal and
arbitration work — with one specific concern about the bill's "nondelegable"
verification duty as currently framed, drawn from research I've conducted on
the practical effects of AI-verification mandates already in force in
California and elsewhere.

The empirical premise behind treating unverified AI-sourced citation error as
categorically more serious than unverified human error is weaker than it may
appear. Human legal work is not the error-free baseline such a standard
implicitly assumes: independent document-review teams in a widely cited study
identified only 49% and 54% of documents they should have produced, agreeing
with each other just 28% of the time. Legal-specific AI research tools, as
distinct from general-purpose chatbots, now perform at or above human
baselines on several measured tasks.

More importantly for this Committee, a uniform, non-delegable, personal
verification duty imposes a fundamentally different cost on a solo
practitioner than on a large firm. The State Bar's own 2025 Biennial Access,
Fairness, and Diversity Report documents that discipline processes already
fall more heavily on solo and small-firm practitioners, and that
thirty-nine percent of solo practitioners carry no malpractice insurance. A
large firm can distribute AI-output verification across an associate bench
in minutes; a solo practitioner performing the same verification personally,
by hand, can lose the entire time and cost benefit that AI assistance was
meant to provide — with a direct effect on the affordability of legal
services for the low- and middle-income clients solo and small-firm lawyers
disproportionately serve.

I respectfully urge the Committee to consider amending the bill to:

1. **Calibrate the verification duty to tool architecture and matter
   stakes**, rather than imposing an identical personal-verification
   standard regardless of whether the underlying tool is a general-purpose
   chatbot or a closed-loop legal research platform grounded in verified
   authority.
2. **Permit verification to be performed under supervision**, consistent
   with how the profession already treats human-sourced legal research
   (e.g., a supervised associate or paralegal verifying citations), rather
   than requiring the signing attorney to personally re-perform the
   verification in every instance.
3. **Pair the duty with a safe-harbor** for practitioners using tools that
   meet a defined reliability benchmark, so the law rewards adoption of
   better-performing tools rather than treating AI use as inherently
   suspect.

I would welcome the opportunity to provide further research or testimony
before the Committee acts, and recognize the very short window remaining
this session.

Respectfully,
